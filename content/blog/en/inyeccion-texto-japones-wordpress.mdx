## Japanese Keyword Hack in WordPress.

Your site is running perfectly.

You log into the dashboard, browse around, and everything loads fine. Then someone tells you: "Why is your website selling sneakers in Japanese?"

You search for your domain on Google and this appears:

- Titles in Japanese characters
- Links to fake stores
- Thousands of pages you never created

This attack has a name: **Japanese Keyword Hack (JKH)**.

And it is one of the most dangerous at an SEO level.

---

### Why it happens

This attack doesn't happen by magic.

It usually occurs due to:

- Outdated plugins
- Abandoned themes
- Weak credentials
- Misconfigured permissions

Once inside, the attacker doesn't break your website.

They do something smarter:  
they use your authority to rank spam.

Google explains this behavior in its official documentation.

They aren't looking to destroy you.  
They are looking to take advantage of your ranking.



---

### What it is

The *Japanese Keyword Hack* is an SEO spam injection based on **cloaking**.

It works like this:

- You see your normal site.
- Googlebot sees thousands of pages in Japanese.

The malware:

- Detects the user-agent (e.g., Googlebot)
- Serves different content depending on who visits
- Generates non-existent dynamic URLs
- Manipulates indexing

Sucuri also analyzes this behavior in detail.

It is an attack designed for search engines, not for humans.

---

### What it affects

The impact is profound:

- Penalty or de-indexing on Google
- Massive loss of organic traffic
- "Hacked site" warnings
- Damage to brand reputation

Additionally, in some cases, attackers add their own emails to Search Console to hide alerts.

This is no longer just malware.  
It is direct manipulation of your digital presence.

---

### Possible solutions

Removing this hack is not as simple as deleting a file and being done with it.

When I handle these cases, I review:

- `.htaccess` file
- WordPress Core integrity
- `wp-config.php` and `index.php`
- Database
- Conditional redirects
- Users and owners in Search Console
- How Google actually sees the site (`site:yourdomain.com`)

Then:

- I replace core files with clean versions
- I remove obfuscated code
- I change all credentials
- I reinforce permissions
- I organize an indexing cleanup

In more serious cases, I recommend server migration and a full environment scan.

Because eliminating symptoms is not enough.
