## Intent and Structure Analysis.

Writing about a topic isn't enough. You have to understand *why* people are searching for it.

Do they want to buy? Do they want to learn? Are they looking for a specific data point? Are they comparing options?

If you don't hit the mark with the intent, it doesn't matter how good your text is. It won't connect.

---

### Why it happens

Often, we create content thinking about what we want to say, not what the user needs to find.

- We write generic articles for keywords that seek specific answers.
- We build product pages when the user is still in the research phase.
- We mix intents on the same page, confusing both the reader and Google.
- We ignore the format the user expects: a list, a tutorial, a comparison.

The result is content that doesn't satisfy, even if it's well-written.

---

### What it is

Analyzing intent and structure means getting inside the head of the searcher.

- I investigate the real queries that lead to your site.
- I classify each search by type: informational, commercial, transactional, or navigational.
- I observe which dominant format exists in the top results.
- I design the page structure that best responds to that need.

It’s not about guessing. It’s about observing data and patterns.

---

### What it affects

When the intent isn't clear, things happen:

- **High Bounce Rate**: the user enters, doesn't find what they expect, and leaves.
- **Low Time on Page**: if it doesn't answer the query fast, they won't stay to read.
- **Low Conversions**: a visitor in the informational phase won't buy even if you put the button right in front of them.
- **Poor Google Ranking**: if your page doesn't resemble the ones Google already has at the top, it will doubt its relevance.

Content loses its effectiveness, no matter how much technical SEO it has.

---

### Possible solutions

When I perform an intent analysis at Alsnippets, this is what I do:

1. **Extract key queries** from Search Console and keyword research tools.
2. **Analyze the SERP** to see what type of content ranks: long articles, product sheets, videos, or lists?
3. **Define the dominant intent** for each target keyword.
4. **Reorganize or create content** adjusting format, tone, and depth to what the user expects.
5. **Review the page structure** so that the answer is clear from the very first paragraph.

This way, every page has a clear purpose and fulfills it.

This analysis is usually complemented by a [semantic site reorganization](/en/reorganizacion-semantica-sitio) so that the structure also helps.

---

### Tips

- **Look at Google's "people also search for"**. There are clues about related intents there.
- **Ask yourself: does this page solve the doubt in 10 seconds?** If not, you're probably not aligned with the intent.
- **Don't force the intent**. If they are looking for information, don't try to sell to them directly. Earn their trust first.
